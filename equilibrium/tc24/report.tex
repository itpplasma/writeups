\begin{abstract}
The TC24 program needs equilibria whose signed fields, safety factor and flux
coordinates retain known accuracy when exported to the later perturbation and
transport codes. The equilibrium solvers KIN6D, public CHEASE, MARS-associated
CHEASE, VMEC++ and DESC have been compared on exact, circular and shaped
axisymmetric problems, prescribed-safety-factor cases and the received TC24
reference. KIN6D's curved cubic finite elements reach the sampled
exact-case targets with a short solve, and its recovered-flux estimator supports
the circular and shaped references. Export studies now exercise actual readers
in the magnetic-coordinate library libneo, the stability codes GPEC and MARS,
and the drift-kinetic codes NEO-RT and NEO-2. Exact-case paths support the working targets inside the sampled plasma.
Prescribed-safety-factor and collaborator-reference TC24 studies are now included.
The slice remains open: MARS inverse iteration fails, TC24 rates and consumer
accuracy are unqualified, and several solver settings miss accuracy or stopping
targets. The report stops before perturbations and transport.
\end{abstract}

\section{Results and scope}
The central result is practical: KIN6D can resolve the exact Grad--Shafranov
test fields efficiently with curved P3 elements. Its sampled passing solve
times are \PthreeAthreeTime{} seconds on the A3 exact boundary and
\PthreeCerfonTime{} seconds on the shaped Cerfon boundary. These times cover
mesh, assembly and solve. They exclude contour readback and therefore cannot
be used as an end-to-end speed ratio against the other codes.

The circular and E4 studies include KIN6D's estimator and readback cost.
Their committed circular timing table favors public CHEASE at the sampled
targets; the E4 table favors KIN6D over both CHEASE variants. A later FortNum
root-finder repair reduces KIN6D readback cost, but refreshed matched-cost CSVs
remain unavailable. The measured table is retained; the separate TC24
performance control below records the later assembly and solve improvements.

\pageheading{Case ladder and conventions}
The ladder separates numerical error from changes in physics and geometry.
The exact polynomial Solov'ev case has a physical closed flux boundary at
$A=3$. The shaped exact case uses the Cerfon--Freidberg construction
\cite{cerfon}, with $\epsilon=0.32$, elongation $\kappa=1.7$ and triangularity
$\delta=0.33$. Its boundary is the exact zero contour; a Miller curve supplies
the shaping constraints, not the entire boundary. Both cases set the on-axis
safety factor through the analytic amplitude. They are distinct from the
exactly circular and explicit Miller cases below.

The forward toroidal cases use $R_0=6.2$ m, $B_0=5.3$ T,
$F_{\rm edge}=R_0B_0$, zero edge pressure and zero edge poloidal flux.
With source scale $q_s=1.5$, their constant laws are
\[
\begin{array}{lll}
\mathrm{E1}: & p'=0,& FF'=-2B_0/q_s,\\
\mathrm{E2}: & FF'=0,&p'=-2B_0/(q_s\mu_0R_0^2).
\end{array}
\]
Here $q_s$ sets the source amplitude; the safety-factor profile is an output.
E1/E2 use exact circles at $A=40,20,10$. Their $A=3.1$ extensions are the two
E3 variants, stored as \texttt{E1\_A3p1} and \texttt{E2\_A3p1}.
E4 uses each law on the explicit boundary
\[
R=R_0+a\cos[\theta+\arcsin(\delta)\sin\theta],\qquad
Z=\kappa a\sin\theta,
\]
with $a=2$ m and the same shaping parameters as above. E4 is an ITER-scale
shape test. Actual TC24 is a separate source problem.

\subsection*{Signed quantities}
All comparisons use SI units and COCOS 3 \cite{cocos}. Physical toroidal angle
$\phi$ increases counterclockwise viewed from $+Z$, with the right-handed
basis $(\mathbf e_R,\mathbf e_\phi,\mathbf e_Z)$. Poloidal flux $\psi$ is
flux per radian. The field and equilibrium equation are
\[
\mathbf B=\nabla\psi\mathbin{\times}\nabla\phi+F\nabla\phi,
\quad B_R=-\psi_Z/R,\quad B_Z=\psi_R/R,\quad B_\phi=F/R,
\]
\[
\Delta^*\psi+\mu_0R^2p'(\psi)+FF'(\psi)=0,\qquad
\Delta^*=\partial_R^2-R^{-1}\partial_R+\partial_Z^2.
\]
The signed full toroidal flux is $\Phi=\int B_\phi\,dR\,dZ$ and
$q=(2\pi)^{-1}d\Phi/d\psi$. The published labels are
$s_{\rm pol}=(\psi-\psi_{\rm axis})/(\psi_{\rm edge}-\psi_{\rm axis})$,
$s_{\rm tor}=\Phi/\Phi_{\rm edge}$ and $\rho_{\rm tor}=\sqrt{s_{\rm tor}}$.
E0--E4 and the TC24 modx03 reference use an axis-minimum flux. The
JINTRAC variant retains its separately declared axis-maximum polarity. No sign, gauge offset beyond the declared edge gauge, field scale or
coordinate alignment is fitted to reduce a comparison error.

CHEASE's native convention is mapped explicitly to the common convention.
The MARS-associated EQDSK path also uses its recorded physical $F/q$ partner
reversal. That reversal must not be copied to the native MARS
\texttt{OUTRMAR} path, whose delivered $F/q$ already have the required signs.

\subsection*{Cylinder and inverse variants}
The periodic cylinder has independent radius $a$ and axial period $L$.
Gold--Hoyle has constant signed right-handed winding; Lundquist supplies a
sheared force-free companion. Finite-aspect-ratio continuation increases the
torus radius while holding the physical period and pitch fixed. Inverse
prescribed-$q$ cases release the forward $FF'$ law and require their own
matching study, delivered in the inverse section below.

\pageheading{Methods and accuracy measures}
KIN6D solves the weak Grad--Shafranov equation on triangles with generic curved
P2 or P3 geometry and field interpolation. Current main supports cubic
profile laws, signed current constraints and prescribed $q$, with their
executed contracts kept separate. The tests predict $L^2$ orders
$p+1$ for flux and $p$ for its field derivatives on smooth, adequately resolved
domains. The recovered field used by the estimator is continuous and belongs
to $H(\mathrm{div})$. Public and MARS-associated CHEASE use bicubic Hermite
finite elements \cite{chease}; their source selectors, normalizations and
export implementations are kept distinct. VMEC++ minimizes constrained MHD
energy on nested Fourier surfaces with radial finite differences. DESC solves
force balance in a Fourier--Zernike representation. The benchmark predictions
are first-order bulk radial convergence for VMEC++ and spectral convergence
for DESC, conditional on boundary representation and native stopping
\cite{panici}.

The exact cases supply analytic flux and fields, with independent contour
quadrature and field-line integration for $q$. Circular and E4 references are
the finest KIN6D P3 solutions. Richardson estimates and the native recovered-flux
majorant assess their error. VMEC++ and DESC receive the reference boundary,
pressure, rotational transform and toroidal flux. Their $q/\Phi$ errors therefore
measure input transfer; their physical field and axis errors remain independent
outputs. The exact-source comparison has the same distinction.

For fixed sources, the energy error and functional estimate have the form
\[
\|e\|_a^2=\int_\Omega R^{-1}|\nabla e|^2\,dR\,dZ
          =\int_\Omega R|\delta\Bpol|^2\,dR\,dZ,
\]
\[
\|e\|_a\ \leq\ \|\sqrt R(\mathbf y-R^{-1}\nabla\psi_h)\|_{L^2}
       +C_A\|f+\nabla\cdot\mathbf y\|_{L^2},
\quad f=\mu_0Rp'+FF'/R.
\]
$C_A$ satisfies $\|v\|_{L^2}\leq C_A\|v\|_a$ on the represented domain.
The continuous identity assumes a conforming field, matching boundary trace
and the stated source. KIN6D evaluates the estimate with ordinary quadrature.
It does not cover boundary displacement, nonlinear source errors or consumer
interpolation automatically. Recovery stopped at an iteration cap can still
produce an admissible finite field; its estimate may be less sharp.

Phase 1/2/4a native comparison norms use 4000 fixed interior points through $s_{\rm pol}=0.98$
weighted by $R$, sampling physical volume. Safety factor uses 40 labels over
$0.05\leq s_{\rm tor}\leq0.98$. Field maxima divide by the reference maximum; $q$ uses the
maximum pointwise relative error. The working targets are $10^{-6}$ for flux
$L^2$, axis, volume and edge toroidal flux; $10^{-5}$ for field $L^2$ and $q$;
and $10^{-4}$ for field maxima. Native stopping is also required. The sample
excludes the outer flux shell; the reconstructed force-balance RMS is reported
without a target. Phase 3 uses 600 interior samples; TC24 uses 1200.
Their source rows and READMEs retain the distinct sampling domains. These targets are provisional until downstream sensitivity
to equilibrium error is measured.

The studies ran locally with one numerical thread and bounded native solves
on a shared Ryzen workstation. Producer DOF counts have different meanings;
time is a single observation under ambient load. Phase 1's KIN6D solve time
excludes readback. Phases 2/4 include its estimator, export and readback; all
exclude common comparison-oracle evaluation and the spectral codes' shared
reference construction. Those distinctions govern every cost statement here.

\pageheading{Periodic-cylinder limit}
The cylinder uses $a=0.62$ m, $L=2\pi\times6.2$ m and
$|B_{\rm axis}|=5.3$ T. Its right-handed fields are
\[
\begin{array}{lll}
\text{Gold--Hoyle}:&B_z=b/(1+\tau^2r^2),&B_\theta=\tau rB_z,\\
\text{Lundquist}:&B_z=bJ_0(\alpha r),&B_\theta=bJ_1(\alpha r).
\end{array}
\]
The primary controls use $q_{\rm RH}=-1.5$ and $\alpha a=-1.5$.
Embedding the cylinder by $x=R-R_{\rm major}$, $y=-Z$ and $z$ along
$+\phi$ gives $q_c=-Lq_{\rm RH}/(2\pi R_{\rm major})$.
The forward toroidal laws fix $F=R_{\rm major}f(\psi/R_{\rm major})$.
The finite-$A$ field approaches the cylinder at first order in $1/A$;
the unfitted first-order correction leaves a second-order remainder.
Integrated flux and scaled winding start at second order.
