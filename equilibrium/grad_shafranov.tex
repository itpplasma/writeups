\section*{Signed field, current and force balance}
\begin{itemize}
\item SI; $(R,\phi,Z)$ is right-handed; $\phi$ increases counter-clockwise viewed from $+Z$. $\psi$ is signed poloidal flux per radian; $F=RB_\phi$. Assume axisymmetry, $R>0$, isotropic pressure, static ideal force balance and sufficiently smooth nested surfaces where $p(\psi),F(\psi)$ are used.
\item Native arrays stay native. Canonical mappings and polarity tests belong to the \href{https://gitlab.tugraz.at/plasma/proj/plasma-sign-conventions/-/blob/main/docs/AXISYMMETRIC_COCOS_CONTRACT.md}{sign/COCOS contract}. Canonical CCW poloidal angle makes $(r,\theta,\phi)$ left-handed (COCOS 3).
\end{itemize}
\begin{align*}
\mathbf B&=\nabla\psi\times\nabla\phi+F\nabla\phi,
& (B_R,B_Z,B_\phi)&=\frac1R(-\psi_Z,\psi_R,F),\\
\ds\psi&=\psi_{RR}-R^{-1}\psi_R+\psi_{ZZ},
&\mu_0j_\phi&=-\ds\psi/R,\quad \mu_0\mathbf j_p=F'\mathbf B_p,\\
\mathbf j\times\mathbf B&=-\frac{\ds\psi+FF'}{\mu_0R^2}\nabla\psi
=\nabla p,
&\boxed{\ds\psi=-\mu_0R^2\pp-FF'.}
\end{align*}
The reduction to surface functions requires its assumptions; a general imported field need not satisfy them. The axis limit is evaluated by regular limiting formulas, not division by a vanishing poloidal field.

\section*{Weak form and its variational functional}
For fixed Dirichlet trace on a bounded poloidal domain $D\subset\{R>0\}$, use $v=0$ on the boundary. Since $\ds\psi/R=\nabla_{RZ}\cdot(\nabla_{RZ}\psi/R)$,
\begin{align*}
\int_D\frac{\nabla\psi\cdot\nabla v}{R}\,dR\,dZ
&=\int_D\left(\mu_0R\pp+\frac{FF'}R\right)v\,dR\,dZ,\\
\mathcal I[\psi]&=\int_D\left[\frac{|\nabla\psi|^2}{2R}-\mu_0Rp(\psi)-\frac{F(\psi)^2}{2R}\right]dR\,dZ,
\qquad\delta\mathcal I=0.
\end{align*}
\begin{itemize}
\item This is the GS variational functional, not a claim about the full dynamical MHD energy principle. Integration by parts and the boundary/domain assumptions are hand derivations here.
\item Constant $\pp,FF'$ give an affine source and coercive linear elliptic problem with fixed trace. Nonlinear profiles do not automatically give a convex functional, a unique branch or a global minimum.
\item KIN6D uses P1 triangles, the $1/R$ weak weight, three-point quadrature, FortFEM meshing/basis and FortNum preconditioned CG. Its numerical geometry is the supplied polygon; continuous-boundary error remains separate.
\end{itemize}

\section*{Exact references: cylinder versus torus}
\textbf{C0: straight, force-free Gold--Hoyle cylinder.} Let $k=1/(qR_0)$ and declare axial period $2\pi R_0$:
\[
 B_z=\frac{B_0}{1+k^2r^2},\quad B_\theta=krB_z,\quad
 \nabla\times\mathbf B=\frac{2k}{1+k^2r^2}\mathbf B,\quad p=0,
 \quad q=\frac{rB_z}{R_0B_\theta}=\frac1{kR_0}.
\]
Its flux analogue is $\psi=R_0B_0\log(1+k^2r^2)/(2k)$, with $\psi_r=R_0B_\theta$. This is not an exact finite-$R$ toroidal GS equilibrium. Uniform $B_z$ plus $B_\theta\propto r$ is not its zero-pressure substitute.

\textbf{E0: exact finite-$R$ GS oracles.} Supply their exact boundary trace:
\begin{align*}
\Psi_C&=C[(R^2-R_0^2)^2+4R^2Z^2],
&\ds\Psi_C&=16CR^2,&\pp&=-16C/\mu_0,&FF'&=0,\\
\Psi_D&=D[R^2\log(R/R_0)-(R^2-R_0^2)/2+Z^2],
&\ds\Psi_D&=4D,&\pp&=0,&FF'&=-4D.
\end{align*}
For the logarithmic family, reconstruct $F^2$ from its declared anchor and check positivity. A polynomial level-set boundary is close to a circle at large aspect ratio; it is not an exact circle. The analytical surface/flux map used for VMEC++ is derived separately in KIN6D theory.

\section*{Forward selectors, inverse selectors and profile meaning}
\begin{itemize}
\item Forward problem: fix LCFS, $\pp(\psi),FF'(\psi),F_{\rm edge},p_{\rm edge}$. Axis flux, total current and $q$ are outputs. E1 has $\pp=0$, $FF'=-2B_0/q_{\rm source}$; E2 has $FF'=0$, $\pp=-2B_0/(q_{\rm source}\mu_0R_0^2)$. Source scale $q_{\rm source}=1.5$ does not prescribe constant $q$.
\item Inverse prescribed-$q$ problem: specify boundary, pressure and an explicit flux/current scale, then release the $F$/current profile. Do not overconstrain the forward problem or silently replace an unsupported inverse mode.
\item Circular cases use $R_0=6.2$ m, $B_0=5.3$ T and $A=10,20,40$; then $A=3.1$, shaping and actual TC24. Finite-$R$ surfaces need not remain concentric circles.
\item Derivative-controlled reconstruction is a named interpretation of tabulated input, not silent source repair:
\end{itemize}
\[
 p(\psi)=p_e+\int_{\psi_e}^{\psi}\pp(u)\,du,\qquad
 F(\psi)=\operatorname{sign}(F_e)\sqrt{F_e^2+2\int_{\psi_e}^{\psi}FF'(u)\,du}.
\]
\begin{itemize}
\item Integrate piecewise-linear derivative segments exactly; extend endpoint derivatives constantly when needed. Reject $F^2\leq0$ or an undefined zero-edge branch. Keep received $p,F$ columns unchanged and report their discrepancy from this reconstruction.
\item Derivative-controlled and raw-column-controlled inputs can define different equilibria. A global COCOS change cannot repair an internally inconsistent derivative tuple.
\end{itemize}

\section*{How to review disagreements}
\begin{itemize}
\item Test both hypotheses: the outlier has a defect, and agreeing codes share an error. Shared genealogy, fixture, parser, COCOS converter or surface mapper reduces independence; do not decide by vote.
\item Classify model/constraint, input/profile, convention and numerical differences before calling a code wrong. Retain native outputs and failed runs; an untested interpretation remains open.
\item Concrete examples: unsigned-$B_p$ winding estimators can share a sign error; CHEASE exterior interpolation can alter reconstructed $q$ without altering native $q$; staircase/Fourier boundary errors can dominate a field comparison.
\item Leonardo GS's initial Picard failure was resolved at unchanged inputs by stronger relaxation. Continuous accuracy and branch uniqueness remain open. VMEC++ refined-boundary failures likewise remain failures, not admitted refined results.
\item Next checks and closure evidence belong in the linked TC24 errata. This writeup closes no unexplained cross-code discrepancy and makes no perturbation/transport claim.
\end{itemize}
